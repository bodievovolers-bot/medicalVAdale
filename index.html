03 — Process Knowledge

This is how I understand RCM (revenue cycle management), step by step: what happens to every patient, in order, from the moment the phone rings to the moment the bill is paid. Watch Carla Diaz, a fictional patient, move through the six steps. The daily tasks at the bottom keep running beside her the whole time.

Carla
Insurance said no? Fix it and resend
1

Call & Booking

Someone calls the clinic. I make sure they are in the right record and book a visit.

  • Answer within three rings and say the clinic's name
  • Chest pain or trouble breathing? Tell them to call 911 and alert the nurse
  • Check who they are with two things: full name and birth date
  • Search twice before making a new record, so nobody ends up with two files
  • Ask five simple questions (why, seen here before, any changes, in person or video, best time), book, and read the details back
Example: Carla Diaz

Carla calls for the first time. There is no record under her name, birth date or phone number, so I make one and book her visit.

Two things checkedNo duplicate recordVisit booked
2

Pre-Visit

Before she comes in, I get everything ready so nothing blocks the visit or the payment later.

  • Send the new-patient forms and check every one is signed
  • Ask if she has a second insurance, so we know which one pays first
  • Check the insurance 1 to 3 days ahead: is it active, and what does it cover?
  • Find out if it needs a doctor's referral or approval before the visit
  • Tell her about how much she will pay (an estimate)
Example: Carla Diaz

Her insurance is active. She still has $100 left to pay on her deductible (the amount a patient pays first each year), then 20% of the rest.

$108estimate = $100 + 20% of the other $40
3

Visit Day

She comes in. I check her in and make sure her record is right.

  • Confirm who she is, and any change to her address or insurance
  • Collect her payment at the desk, in the safe payment system only
  • Open today's visit inside her existing file, never a second file
  • Make sure the doctor signs the visit note, because no signed note means no bill
Example: Carla Diaz

Carla arrives and pays at check-in. The doctor sees her and signs the note.

$100paid at the desk
4

Claim

After the visit, I turn what happened into a bill for the insurance company. It is called a claim.

  • Turn the doctor's note into codes: one for why she came, one for what was done
  • Never guess a code. If unsure, ask
  • Check the claim for mistakes and fix them before sending
  • Send it through a clearinghouse (a middle system that checks and forwards claims) and keep the "accepted" report as proof
Example: Carla Diaz

A $230 claim goes out clean and the insurance company accepts it.

$230billed to insurance
File receivedClaim accepted
5

Payments & Denials

The insurance pays. I record the money and fix any problem.

  • Match the bank deposit to the insurance's payment report
  • Record three things: what insurance paid, what the clinic must write off, and what the patient owes
  • Check the math: the three parts must add up to the full bill
  • If the insurance says no (a denial), read the reason, fix it, and resend before the deadline
  • Never bill the patient for the write-off
Example: Carla Diaz

The $230 bill splits into three parts, and they add up exactly.

  • Insurance paid $32
  • Clinic writes off $90 (never billed to her)
  • Patient share $108
6

Patient Billing

Now only what the patient truly owes is left. I bill that, nothing more.

  • Bill the patient only after all insurance has finished
  • Bill only her own share, never the write-off
  • Explain the bill in simple words if she calls
  • Close the account when the balance is zero
Example: Carla Diaz

She owed $108 and already paid $100 at the desk, so only the rest is billed.

$108her share−$100paid at desk=$8left to pay
Closed · $0

Words used above, in plain English

Insurance eligibility
Checking that a patient's insurance is active and what it covers.
Deductible
The amount a patient pays first each year before insurance starts paying.
Claim
The bill the clinic sends to the insurance company.
Denial
The insurance says no to a claim. I find out why and fix it.
Write-off
The part of the bill the clinic agreed not to charge. The patient never pays it.
Prior authorization
Approval from the insurance before an expensive service, like an MRI.
